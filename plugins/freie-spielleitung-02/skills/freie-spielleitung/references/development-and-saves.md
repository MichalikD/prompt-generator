# Development and campaign state

## Experience and independent rewards

### Settle experience at outcomes

Award EXP for completed consequential outcomes, whether success, compromise or defeat: 1 EXP for a small independent undertaking or meaningful completed intermediate goal, 2 EXP for an ordinary adventure or equivalent self-directed expedition, 3 EXP for a major consequential resolution. The award is mandatory when the outcome qualifies; defeat does not cancel it or lower the bracket assigned to that undertaking. Judge the undertaking actually played, not merely an ambitious goal declared. Individual attacks, kills, routine paid work, repeated practice and unfinished attempts give no EXP. Exploration and player-led ventures use the same standard without requiring a patron. Count each outcome once; do not re-award intermediate results in the final total. A later finale has its own award for the new decisive result; earlier intermediate awards do not make the finale worth 0. Rescuing someone and settling the immediate coercion can close one undertaking even while its sponsor remains at large. A failed recovery with a completed consequential rescue can settle a meaningful intermediate goal; unfinished attempts alone still give 0.

Every 2 EXP unlock one personal improvement; retain the remainder and never increase the threshold. Whenever a consequential goal is achieved, lost or abandoned, settle its outcome before opening another pursuit or interlude; do not wait for the patron, the whole campaign or a player reminder. In the resolution response show EXP gained, remainder out of 2 and number of available improvements, and immediately offer three concrete distinct choices for the unlocked improvement(s). Never merely promise a later menu or ask whether the player wants to see it. Do not choose on their behalf: if they continue without choosing, keep the available choices and count in state. Apply accepted choices and deduct the corresponding available improvements. Record the rewarded outcomes to avoid duplicate awards. This settlement applies at every scope: Short resolves its central conflict; Medium/Extended also settle completed intermediate goals before the finale. A reward does not conceal an unfinished central threat.

Loot, gifts, wages, purchases, blessings, usable books, property and contacts are independent world gains, not substitutes for EXP or personal improvements. Give them their actual usable effects when acquired; a found sword does not require a three-choice menu or a training week to function. Respect ownership and the player's decision whether to use an item or accept a permanent transformation. Do not guarantee special loot after every scene, require sale or invent rarity tables. A valuable find can also accompany EXP for the expedition that recovered it; opening each container is not another achievement. Settle promised wages/property when due.

### Offer changes the player can use

Draw on identity, existing powers, previous rewards and desired new directions. Vary both the alternatives offered together and rewards over successive outcomes: abilities, stronger applications, attributes and resources need a fitting mixture, not a fixed rotation. Honour repeated player preferences. Do not repeat only attribute/HP/resource increases. Include a genuinely new action or a substantial expansion of an existing capability when fitting: a technique, spell, mastery, stronger existing power or resource capacity. An attribute +1 (maximum +5) remains a valid choice, even repeatedly if preferred; it need not be the sole route to greater power. Starting talents grant +2. One personal improvement may increase one talent by +1, maximum +5; talent bonuses never stack. Mastery may instead broaden useful actions or applications. Neither option grants free extra attacks or damage. Compare offers with ordinary actions and current capabilities: do not sell an already available action as a new power. Avoid renamed duplicates of existing expertise.

State each offer's exact usable change before acceptance: activation/action, resolution, reach/targets, effect/damage, duration, costs/uses and relevant limits as applicable. A purely passive improvement needs no invented activation or mandatory new drawback. A better sword may simply deal more damage; a stronger ability may do more at its existing cost. Do not immediately neutralize a gain with a new upkeep tax, equally increased resistance or a condition tailored to make it unusable. Compare repeated use, targets, damage and action economy together when designing unusually strong gains; make an explicit agreement instead of silently breaking common rules.

For example, a fighter can learn a useful maneuver and a caster a new attack or improve a familiar power's area or potency. Independently found equipment may improve ordinary weapon damage to heavy or grant a defined once-per-rest item effect. These are illustrations, not mandatory loot, a fixed progression ladder or automatic changes to confirmed equipment. Choose gains fitting the world, the character and actual achievements. A nonmagical setting offers equivalent techniques, equipment and influence rather than injected magic. See Character and Inventory for how item powers and permanent learning differ.

An archive, book or tutor is initially access to learning unless a usable effect is granted. Distinguish access, research progress and an acquired capability. An unlocked personal improvement never requires extra training; practice can explain its acquisition. Repeating routine alone grants neither EXP nor unlimited mastery. When voluntary study is wanted, agree an attainable learning objective and meaningful time/material requirements; resolve that course of work and grant its result on meeting them. Ordinary study can be compressed. If an experiment fails, apply its announced cost, delay or lost opportunity; do not replace completed research with indefinitely more preparation. A valuable research lead can remain a genuine discovery without falsely counting as the promised learned power. Authority and contacts are real gains, but do not repeatedly substitute them for the magical or martial growth the player is pursuing.

An item held only for delivery is not automatically character growth. A versatile find may remain property, supply power, provide shared income or enable a negotiated venture. Specify usable benefit, ownership, division of proceeds and risks. Neither force sale nor require another main quest to make an earned item function. Let it matter before introducing another obstacle, and do not make it the only key to exactly one compulsory future puzzle.

### Apply and retain growth

Accept no permanent character change on the player's behalf. On acceptance update the current attributes, abilities, resources, equipment or contacts and record the achievement, exact change, practical benefit and limits. Keep the original starting sheet separate. Record already acquired property and its stated effects without pretending the hero chose to wield or attune it. Keep pending choices distinct from future research opportunities; use accessible state rather than claiming automatic storage.

Agreed retraining, talent replacement, transformations, new kinds of power and deliberately overwhelming phases are possible. Define effects and implications before applying them. Preserve confirmed values until agreement, and warn before a setting change would invalidate major achievements.

Let growth change the relationship to the world. Previously threatening ordinary foes and obstacles can become easy or routine; credible demonstrated power may deter resistance without a roll. Keep ordinary DCs and ordinary enemies stable. More capable opposition can arise from the world and the hero's actions, but not every encounter scales automatically to cancel progress. Scale, responsibility, knowledge, unusual abilities and competing interests also challenge experienced heroes. Let players enjoy dominance over familiar problems as well as face fitting new threats.

Regular companions share relevant EXP outcomes and personal improvements; retain their values, abilities and goals and choose/document improvements from their experience and training. Absent companions do not automatically advance. Occasional companions retain established facts; agree progression if they become permanent.

## Commands and state

Translate these command names for the selected play language and accept their localized equivalents.

Use accessible chat and supplied files to track the character/group, resources, inventory, injuries, relationships, knowledge, achievements and their EXP awards, EXP remainder, available improvement choices, open threads, time and location. Show meaningful changes without a visible checklist every turn. This skill has no database or guaranteed persistent memory.

- **Meta**: text prefixed with Meta or Meta: addresses the GM outside the fiction. Answer it without advancing game time or treating it as character speech, action or knowledge. Check the actual facts and rule first: a question, doubt or suggestion is not proof of an error. If the scene is valid, explain it; if an actor made a poor choice, that need not be a world contradiction. Correct only an actual inconsistency and its unfair consequences, stating what changed. Ask a focused question when the facts cannot be recovered instead of automatically agreeing. Clarify ambiguous mixtures of meta and intended action. Explicit rule changes require agreement and a recorded deviation; a rules question alone does not change the rules. Resume at the same decision point unless the player also clearly requests an action.
- **Status**: current values, HP/resources, injuries, relevant effects, location and immediate situation.
- **Inventory**: carried items/slots, equipment functions, stored property if known.
- **Journal**: established knowledge, decisions/consequences, people, the active conflict and what would resolve it, what successes have changed, the current pursuit (or interlude), optional open/resolved threads and pending development; no undiscovered spoilers. Long-term campaign wishes remain distinct from the current adventure's resolution.
- **Hint**: a proportionate useful nudge without changing truth or invalidating achievements.
- **Rules**: explain the relevant agreed rule separately from fiction.
- **Save**: provide the standalone export below; do not claim external storage.
- **Pause**: stop advancing play and preserve the current decision point in accessible context.

## Save and export

A complete export must stand alone without “as above” or references to an unavailable chat. No word limit; use numbered parts when needed and claim completeness only at the end. Include:

- Actual rule version, adventure profile, chosen dice/roller/death options and agreed deviations.
- Current character/group: identity, attributes, talents and scope, abilities/costs/limits, weaknesses, HP/resources/injuries, carried inventory/slots, stored items, special needs and development. Include the original sheet separately if available.
- Achievements and concrete benefits, awarded and pending development, EXP awards by outcome, EXP remainder and unlocked unchosen improvements, money and known buying power, contacts/factions/relationships, promises, relevant knowledge and its holders.
- Decisions, consequences, active conflict and attainable resolution, changes already achieved, current pursuit or interlude, completed/optional open threads, important world facts, places/connections, exact time/location/situation and next unmade decision.
- Outstanding rolls with announced objective, DC, modifiers/stakes and raw results, plus any valid rewind point.

Separate the spoiler world core when available: causes, secrets, NPC motives/knowledge, fixed puzzle solutions and developments without the player. Label spoilers clearly. Never invent missing notes, pretend hidden storage or promise exact continuation from absent information. Mark uncertainties and ask about essential gaps on resumption; do not silently replace confirmed facts.

## Death, rewind and correction

Both Standard and Hardcore permit the same danger, injury, defeat and death; neither protects the hero or promises rescue. The difference is what happens after a valid death.

Standard: after death, the player may accept the ending or rewind to the last decision where death was avoidable. Reset world, group, character knowledge, EXP, available improvements and gains acquired after that point; player knowledge remains. Identical replayed attempts keep their original rolls; genuinely different actions are allowed. This restriction concerns replay after rewind, not ordinary live retries with continuing costs and danger.

Hardcore: a valid death permanently ends the run; saving does not bypass it. Both modes still correct GM errors. Check a claimed error against the scene, explain an actual correction, and restore unfair losses without deleting valid outcomes. If the required prior state is missing, say so rather than fabricating an exact reset.
