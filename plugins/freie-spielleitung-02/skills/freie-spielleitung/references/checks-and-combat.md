# Checks and combat

## Decide whether to roll

Roll uncertain actions with meaningful consequences. Clear actions succeed; impossible actions do not become possible through a lucky die. Use relevant expertise for basic knowledge. Chosen check frequency changes opportunities for risks, not DC or mandatory roll counts. Tone does not change numerical difficulty.

For conversation, first identify what the player is trying to obtain and their offer, argument or approach. A short declaration is sufficient; do not require an eloquent speech. If the approach is still missing, ask what they say or do and wait, without simultaneously requesting a die. If it is already supplied, request only the raw roll when one is needed; do not end that request with an NPC asking the player to persuade them again. For persuasion, distinguish a person already willing to act from an uncertain attempt to overcome resistance. An eloquent speech supplies arguments and leverage; it does not alone establish that a listener abandons revenge, power, safety or loyalty. When the response remains uncertain and consequential, announce one fitting check before deciding it. On failure, enact the announced refusal, cost or escalation rather than praising the speech into success. A roll resolves the named remaining uncertainty, such as overcoming mistrust or obtaining a concession, not the entire conversation or the player's decision. An NPC's own acceptable offer needs the player's acceptance, not a roll to make that NPC agree to it. Do not add a roll to a settled agreement, make a roll bypass a person's actual limits, or roll simply because no dice have appeared recently.

Before a check, state the intended objective, attribute, applicable talent/equipment, DC and foreseeable stakes in a meta note using the selected language. Do not reveal hidden facts just to announce risk. Preparation must have a stated effect: it permits the action, lowers DC, or gives advantage. Name that effect when announcing the check; if DC is lower, show the change when the baseline is known, otherwise explain that the chosen DC already includes the preparation. Praise alone is not a mechanical effect. Preparation that removes meaningful uncertainty can remove the need to roll. Genuine help can do the same; no invented support +2. Count a cause once.

Resolve the whole declared action. Use multiple stages only if they involve distinct decisions/stakes, not every grip and step. A successful preparation does not merely unlock another identical check; carry its effect into the actual objective. If the player declares several routine actions, compress them until the next meaningful uncertainty or decision.

## Formula and dice

D20 + attribute + one applicable talent (starting +2; agreed advancement up to +5) + one equipment bonus (at most +2) ≥ DC: success.

| DC | Difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| 8 | easy |
| 11 | normal |
| 14 | demanding |
| 17 | hard |
| 20 | exceptional |

Use the table as the baseline. If actual preparation or circumstances change a task DC, show the baseline and reason (for example 14 → 11 after proper preparation). Do not silently replace it with arbitrary intermediate task DCs. Target defense is a separate derived value and can legitimately be 12, 13, 15 or another intermediate number; label it defense, not a new task difficulty.

Advantage: 2D20, take the higher. Disadvantage: 2D20, take the lower. Multiple sources do not stack; advantage and disadvantage cancel. No double reward/penalty for one cause. Natural 1/20 receive plausible effects, not automatic death, impossible feats or unannounced critical damage.

With player rolls, request raw dice for their character, wait, then calculate; do not request a pre-added total or invent the player’s raw roll. Keep this division after downtime and in long noncombat sequences too; clarify an ambiguous supplied total rather than adding its bonuses again. The GM handles NPCs/enemies using an actual random tool if available. Without one, label chat rolls as simulated once. Never pretend a tool was used, alter a result or reroll for a preferred outcome.

Open display includes enemies and raw arithmetic separately from fiction, e.g. “D20 12+Dexterity 3+Talent 2=17 ≥ DC14: success.” Show both advantage/disadvantage dice. With compact display, show outcome and changes; provide details on request. The previously announced stakes and DC remain binding. Equality is success: a total of 14 against defense 14 hits. A natural 1 does not authorize an unannounced damage rule or arbitrary catastrophe; apply the agreed check and its stated plausible consequences.

## Failure and retries

Failure changes the situation according to the announced stakes: harm, lost time, exposure, spent resources, changed position, relationship damage, a missed opportunity, or success at a price. Apply the consequence promptly and continue from the changed state. Failure can end an attempt or an adventure. Do not replace it with a new prerequisite and another near-identical roll until safe success is inevitable.

Same-method retries are allowed when fiction permits. A missed rope throw can be thrown again while the victim continues sinking; the throw need not be cosmetically redesigned. Each attempt costs the time/action/resources the situation requires, and consequences accumulate. If nothing can change and no consequence is possible, do not farm rolls: resolve the routine or state why no further attempt is available. A definitive failure may close an opportunity; a retry is not an entitlement.

When the announced consequence is eventual success at a time/resource cost, apply that cost and finish the declared action without another roll. Otherwise stop before committing the player to a new approach or further risk. If preparation, help or a resisting actor's actual behaviour changes the situation, update uncertainty and stakes visibly. Preserve the preparation already completed instead of resetting it. Distinct phases need distinct decisions or consequences; do not turn one repair into a sequence of hidden prerequisites. Do not invent and execute the character's improved solution after a failed check. NPCs may suggest or carry out their own plausible actions without choosing for the player.

Apply meaningful harm, loss and death when the fiction and resolution warrant them, in both Standard and Hardcore. State foreseeable stakes for the character before a roll; this does not require advance consent to every hostile event or disclosure of hidden threats. Resolve surprises through the actual situation and applicable checks, not automatic defeat. Preserve the result of a check and the actual benefit of precautions. New events or enemy actions may create new danger, but do not retroactively change announced stakes, an established anchor or a completed success. Correct an inconsistent ruling and restore unfair costs.

## Combat

NPCs can initiate combat, including an ambush or attack during conversation. Fighting, negotiation, flight, deception and surrender are attempts under actual circumstances, not guaranteed exits. Decide behaviour from motives and capabilities rather than protecting either side. Explain relevant positions, cover, reach and visible intentions before consequential choices.

### Establish the encounter

At the transition from a failed ambush, negotiation or exploration into combat, establish the current actors and order once. Retain surprise already gained and actions already spent; a failed approach does not grant an unannounced extra enemy attack. Resolve every creature's attacks, including the enemy's, as attack roll against target defense; ordinary statements such as "I parry and then attack" do not replace this with a player defense check or grant a free counterattack. Fix each active creature's HP, attack bonus, defense, damage, movement and special effects before resolving its attacks. Retain remaining turns and temporary-effect expiry as well as HP; a quiet ally still guards, moves, acts or deliberately waits for a stated reason rather than disappearing from the encounter. In open display, show these values for visible combatants and remaining HP after damage, including companions. Hide genuinely unknown abilities until relevant; do not invent or adjust values to fit a roll. For newly designed ordinary humanoid opposition, 4 HP (fragile), 6 HP (trained) and 10 HP (formidable) are useful baselines, not universal species values. Justify stronger creatures and substantial exceptions. Assess numbers, actions, damage and the hero's available capabilities together; describe recognizable overwhelming danger without making every fight fair or automatically scaling foes to the hero.

Default defense = 10+Dexterity, plus 1 for appropriate armor. Each turn grants one action and up to 10 m movement, split before and after the action; dash costs the action and adds up to 10 m. Respect terrain, obstacles and actual reach (ordinarily 1.5 m, or the confirmed weapon's reach). A simple incidental interaction such as drawing an accessible weapon can accompany a turn; picking a dropped weapon up costs the action. Do not invent free hands or an extra attack from holding two weapons. Removing an embedded projectile is not a free heal or a new injury penalty.

Use the fiction for a clear order; otherwise each participant rolls D20+Dexterity without talent/equipment bonuses, highest first. Reroll only tied participants. Keep that order until circumstances actually change. Finish all earlier turns before requesting the player's action and all later turns before the next round. Do not restart each round at the player or let one actor act twice. On interruption, retain round, current actor and remaining turns. A compact end-of-round summary of HP, active effects, positions and next actor is enough; ordinary narration need not repeat every stat.

### Before each combat decision

Keep a small working combat state in accessible context: round/order/current actor, positions, current/max HP, current defense, attack formula, damage band, effects with expiry, reaction availability and limited uses. In open display, show visible targets' current/max HP and defense before a tactical choice. Refresh changed values rather than repeating whole sheets. Do not promise hidden state storage.

At each own-turn action choice add a short reminder of normal / controlled / risky attack (with their modifiers), defend / manoeuvre / ready / dash, movement and fitting available abilities with remaining uses where limited. Add a relevant condition such as standing up costing the action. These are reminders, not an exhaustive menu: free ideas remain valid. Explain details when needed, not in a new full rules block for every declared action. At a reaction trigger, offer the available player choice immediately before advancing; honour already declared use or refusal without asking again. Do not skip it because the ordinary turn would follow.

Resolve each attack in this order, for every actor:

1. Announce target, complete attribute/talent/equipment/style formula, defense, advantage/disadvantage and foreseeable effects. Retain the selected damage category and die with that attack.
2. Obtain the attack roll under the selected roller policy. In player-roll mode, request only the hero's raw dice and wait; resolve NPC and enemy dice yourself with the available-tool/simulation procedure. Never wait for permission or a player prompt to finish an NPC turn.
3. Determine hit. A miss has no effective damage. On a hit, resolve that category's selected damage die under Optional Dice; use fixed damage only when its die is not selected. For NPCs resolve attack and damage in the same response; for player dice stop only where their raw roll is still needed.
4. Show damage and HP subtraction, apply any resulting incapacity/effect, then advance through the remaining actors until the next unmade player decision. Do not declare a target defeated, give its last words or start the next turn while damage is outstanding.

Every check includes its attribute even when a talent applies: D20 + Dexterity 2 + talent 2 is +4, not +2. A positive display label must match the numerical comparison and enacted outcome. Do not abbreviate away the attribute or decide an NPC hit in prose. A nearby failed shot may meet armor, cover or evasion rather than fly absurdly wide; close range alone is not an automatic hit. Deliberately aiming at a small component needs a stated useful effect and difficulty before commitment; do not demand it for damage already possible with an ordinary attack.

### Attack styles

Choose a style before rolling a single ordinary melee or ranged attack. An undeclared style means Normal; do not choose Controlled or Risky for the player. The style modifier is separate from the capped equipment bonus. Damage stays the same for all three styles, including selected D4/D6/D8 variation:

| Style | Attack | Effect / cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal | unchanged | Normal damage, no extra effect. |
| Controlled | −2 | On a hit, normal damage and Off Balance. |
| Risky | +2 | Normal damage; own defense −2 until the start of own next turn, even on a miss. |

Off Balance: the target's next attack or offensive combat manoeuvre has disadvantage. Consume it on that attempted roll, hit or miss; otherwise it expires at the end of the target's next turn. It does not stack or force a stabilization action. The target may choose another useful action. A controlled hit does not additionally stun, disarm or knock down. No older style-based damage modifier or automatic advantage on the creator's next attack applies. Do not automatically combine a style with a non-attack manoeuvre, special ability or reaction attack; the agreed special rule governs those.

Risky's defense reduction does not stack with another use of the same style. A natural 1 or 20 does not override the numerical comparison or add critical damage.

### Actions and physical states

Ordinary attacks and the following substantial actions each cost the action. Other plausible tactics remain possible; establish costs, useful effect and expiry before the roll. For resisted non-attack physical manoeuvres use a fitting attribute and talent against an announced task DC; 14 is the baseline for evenly matched alert fighters. Use the normal DC ladder and explain relevant changes. Resolve resistance once, not with a second roll to undo success. A named body part alone adds no free disabling effect or permanent injury.

- **Defend:** perceived attacks against the defender have disadvantage until the start of the defender's next turn. Movement remains available; ordinary narration of dodging grants no extra defense roll. This can protect a retreat but does not cancel an opportunity attack.
- **Ready:** name one concrete action and an observable trigger. Before own next turn, perform it at the trigger using the reaction; it expires at the next turn if unused. No extra full turn or movement. A readied shot at an emerging target resolves while it is exposed, never through solid cover.
- **Knock down:** a successful suitable physical check makes the target prone; no normal attack damage. **Stand up:** costs the action, leaves normal movement available and triggers no opportunity attack. There is no separate hasty-standing option.
- **Prone:** own melee attacks have disadvantage; incoming melee attacks have advantage. Crawl at half speed. Incoming ranged attacks from more than 10 m have disadvantage when the smaller exposed silhouette matters. Lying down alone does not penalize own shooting or make someone hidden; nearby melee pressure, an agreed injury or unsuitable handling can matter. A prone creature may act instead of standing.
- **Hold:** needs an appropriate free hand and a successful physical check. Neither participant can move away while the hold is maintained; appropriate attacks and other actions remain possible. Escape costs the action and a fitting Strength/Dexterity check. Voluntary release is free. No automatic damage or universal helplessness.
- **Push:** a successful physical check moves the target up to 3 m if size and obstacles permit; no normal damage or opportunity attack from that forced movement.
- **Disarm:** a successful suitable check drops one held item within 1 m of its holder; no normal damage. Picking it up costs the action.

Keep posture, cover, detection and movement restrictions separate. Multiple causes of advantage/disadvantage never stack. Ordinary HP loss does not add an unagreed wound penalty or make an otherwise able creature unable to stand; optional lasting injuries use their separately agreed rules.

### Reactions, retreat and useful techniques

Each creature has one reaction, initially available and refreshed at the start of its own turn. Opportunity attacks, ready actions and special reaction abilities share it. When a visible enemy voluntarily leaves melee reach, an available reaction permits one normal melee attack immediately before departure. Only an actual appropriate melee attack can be used, without a freely chosen attack style. Forced movement, standing up or turning do not trigger it. Retreat uses normal movement and optionally dash or defend; no separate disengage action. Explain the foreseeable reaction risk before committing to retreat. Pursuit and other attacks still use actual order, range and cover; no additional pursuit damage after that danger was already resolved.

Ordinary misses grant no free counterattack. Confirmed techniques can provide one with their actual cost and trigger. Counterstrike and Trip Hook below are optional technique examples, not universal starting abilities. When newly chosen with a per-combat limit, propose one use each per combat and record that limit explicitly. Other characters retain their own stated uses and recovery; never refill all ability resources merely because a new fight starts. Define the boundary and recovery on a new limited technique before confirmation, including whether it is per combat or per full rest. A continuing encounter does not refresh per-combat uses because a round, phase or opponent changes.

If explicitly chosen, **Trip Hook** adds knockdown after a successful melee hit on the user's own turn, with normal damage, no second roll and no extra action/reaction. It cannot trigger from a reaction attack. Offer the decision after the hit if not predeclared. **Counterstrike** permits a normal melee attack as a reaction when a perceived enemy melee attack misses and the attacker is still in reach. Spend the use on activation even if the counter misses. Neither is granted retroactively to any confirmed figure.

### Cover and turn flow

Cover requires an actual reachable obstruction and is directional: **none** gives no protection; **partial** gives disadvantage to attacks through it, no flat defense bonus; **full** blocks direct attacks through the solid obstruction. Concealment such as foliage need not stop a projectile. Check the actual line from attacker to target before rolling; do not move scenery or invent cover afterwards. Explain unclear layout without charging an action or roll. A body is not automatically safe cover or an automatic recipient of a missed shot; state real collateral risks first.

Reaching or leaving cover uses actual remaining movement. Firing from full cover requires an exposed position; return is possible with remaining movement. Enemies can reposition or ready a shot on their own turns, not gain an arbitrary extra attack. Going prone is not a declaration of cover from every direction.

Resolve whole declared turns within their allowances, then automatically finish every ally's and opponent's remaining turns until the next player turn, required raw roll or genuine reaction decision. Never require “continue” to execute an NPC turn. Allies act, guard or deliberately wait according to their remit; do not invent the hero's reaction choice. The player may still declare unused movement before their turn ends. Preserve current actor, remaining turns and effects across an interruption. Apply successful agreed effects: a disarm is not narrated as “the weapon slips but is retained,” and an opening cannot be erased by an unchanged enemy action. Ordinary opponents remain ordinary as the hero grows.

Base damage remains light 2, ordinary 3, heavy 4 symmetrically; use selected category dice before applying HP changes. Agreed exceptional effects can differ. No unannounced critical multipliers. Mounts/vehicles need their actual target values, not the rider's defense.

## Vehicles and mounted weapons

A turret, siege engine or vehicle uses its own agreed weapon and target properties, not automatically the operator's personal weapon damage or defense. Before committing to the first consequential use, state the operator's fitting check and bonuses, target defense or task DC, reach, damage/effect scale, resources and the result of disabling or destroying the target. Use the normal attack/damage gate and selected category dice if the ordinary damage bands fit. If vehicle-scale damage does not fit, agree a compact separate resolution before rolling; do not silently invent hull percentages, free extra attacks or cinematic damage after the result. Keep vehicle damage distinct from passenger harm. A vehicle-specific deviation does not change personal combat rules.

## Incapacitation, injuries and recovery

At 0 HP a creature is incapacitated. Determine and state whether the established attack and circumstances actually killed them or left them alive; 0 alone is not automatic death. A lethal injury, fire, predation or a deliberate finishing attack can kill. Explain foreseeable lethal danger before a risky check. Apply the selected death/rewind procedure only after actual death. Do not preserve every defeated enemy or guarantee capture, rescue or survival.

A living incapacitated creature needs stabilization and safe care before resting. Ordinary first aid with suitable means and no meaningful uncertainty succeeds; an uncertain urgent rescue uses a check with a specific stated consequence. Stabilization removes the immediate dying danger without restoring HP. Do not invent a separate sequence of rolls for every bandage or a hidden bleeding clock. Once stable, use the same recovery as for other creatures.

Default recovery is symmetric for player characters, companions and enemies: a safe uninterrupted short rest of at least one hour restores +2 HP once between full rests; a safe uninterrupted rest of at least eight hours restores full HP. A stabilized creature can recover this way from 0 HP. Ability resources recover according to their confirmed sheet; if an NPC resource is relevant, define its recovery too. A watch schedule can permit full rest when the creature actually gets the required sleep. First aid does not create extra HP unless an agreed healing effect says so.

Ordinary wounds and pain explain lost HP; they impose no separate penalty or slower recovery. After HP recovery, residual marks or soreness need not hinder play. Carry recovery through compressed days for NPCs as well as the hero; do not repeatedly describe a disabling wound without applying elapsed care and the agreed recovery. Lasting injuries are an optional agreed rule: define trigger, effect, treatment and recovery before using it, apply it consistently to relevant creatures, and distinguish it from HP. Do not suddenly keep NPCs crippled for days while the same wounds disappear overnight for the hero. Default play adds no infection, mandatory surgery or wound-treatment minigame.

## Powers, damage and the environment

Apply the confirmed ability's stated effect and cost. For a non-damaging illusion, feint or control attempt, establish the intended target response and useful effect before the roll or resource commitment. If a confirmed power leaves this unresolved, agree the missing application briefly rather than silently changing the sheet. Resolve meaningful resistance once with an announced fitting check. Carry success through the actual turn order and state when its effect ends: a misdirected action, changed position, an opportunity for an ally or disadvantage on an affected attack can fit the circumstances. Do not narrate a successful distraction and then let its target perform the very action it was meant to disrupt unchanged. An ally using the opening may be the actual gain; no extra hero action or automatic lost enemy turn is implied. A target may recognize an implausible illusion or interact with it on its turn, with consequences consistent with the announced effect, not automatic cancellation of a paid success. For a damaging spell or other power, use the normal D20 + relevant attribute + one talent + one equipment bonus against target defense unless the agreed ability specifies a different check. Use the same default damage bands as weapons: light 2, ordinary 3, heavy 4. These are defaults, not a universal limit on explicitly agreed exceptional powers. State and justify exceptional damage/scale before use. Define target count/area, range, cost and any persistence on the sheet before confirmation; do not improvise a free area attack or silently strip an agreed one down to harmless utility.

An area power costs its agreed action and resources once per use, not once per victim. For an ordinary instantaneous area attack, make one attack roll and compare its total with each exposed target's defense; apply the agreed damage to those hit, respecting any separately agreed resolution. Affected allies are not exempt unless the ability says so. State relevant cover, exposure, targets and foreseeable collateral danger before the player commits. A missed attack does not itself reverse a separately established environmental effect; nor does a damaging attack ignite every nearby object automatically. Fire spreads through suitable fuel and conditions over time; falling debris, smoke and other hazards follow coherent causes. Explain any additional hazard mechanic before enforcing it. Use one defined resolution per consequence rather than a second arbitrary roll to undo success. Fixed damage and any selected damage dice apply consistently to spells and weapons in the relevant damage band.

Keep narrative scope distinct from a numeric check bonus. A powerful ability can change what is possible, remove the need for a roll or reach more targets without granting an invented universal +2. Ordinary success remains possible without a new disadvantage invented to neutralize the power. Apply costs even on failure when the confirmed ability says so; clarify an unspecified material cost or resolution before commitment.

## Optional dice

At combat setup retain the selected dice by damage category. After each hit, if that category's die is selected, request the player's raw damage die and stop before damage, incapacitation, or the next actor's turn; obtain NPC damage through the same available-tool/simulation procedure as their attacks. One message may request attack and damage raw dice together only if it explicitly says the damage die is used on a hit; ignore it on a miss. Never apply base damage first and roll later. A selected D6 means an ordinary hit requires D6 resolution for every side, including spells. If only D6 is selected, heavy damage remains fixed 4; weapon profiles do not activate unselected dice. For an instantaneous area attack roll damage once per use and apply it to all targets hit unless the confirmed power states otherwise.

Use only selected extra dice; otherwise fixed base damage. Explain purpose/table before rolling. Follow the chosen roller/display rules, without attribute/talent modifiers for these extra rolls. Attribute checks remain D20.

- Selected D4 for light, D6 for ordinary, D8 for heavy damage: if roll ≤ floor(W/4), base−1; if roll > W−floor(W/4), base+1; otherwise base. Apply symmetrically to both sides. D4: 1→1 damage, 2–3→2, 4→3. D6: 1→2 damage, 2–5→3, 6→4. D8: 1–2→3 damage, 3–6→4, 7–8→5. These are variations around base damage, not full raw-die damage and not a new damage-0 system. Selecting one damage die does not activate the other categories.
- Selected D10: ten similarly valuable finds, with 0=10 when rolled alone.
- Selected D12: twelve world events, not mandatory detours to extend an adventure.
- Selected D% requires D10: tens 00–90 plus units 0–9; 00+0=100. Announce the chance first; result ≤ chance triggers the event.

Use selected find/event/chance dice when there is a genuine unresolved choice among suitable outcomes, not only in combat. Establish the table or chance before requesting the roll; do not use them to redraw a fixed investigation truth, contradict successful avoidance or create a mandatory extra adventure. Selection makes the option available, not a per-adventure quota.

## Slim weapon profiles

A newly created weapon can carry `weapon:{damage,range,handling,feature,limits}` in inventory. Damage is light/ordinary/heavy; the other fields describe effective reach, hands/space, one useful distinction and its cost or restriction. These fields are authoritative for that agreed item. General description, quantity, carrying slots and check bonus remain separate. Empty/0 weapon range is not unlimited reach. A profile never activates a die not selected by the player. A separately agreed item damage modifier is stated in feature, applied once in addition to the band result (after its selected die variation), and shown in arithmetic; it does not increase the check bonus or create another attack. Existing confirmed weapons without profiles keep their stated properties; do not infer a new template from the name. Agree missing consequential properties before their first relevant use.

As creation proposals, a pistol is compact and one-handed with moderate short-range damage; a shotgun is heavy close-range single-target damage with bulk and reload costs; a rifle can cover greater distance but needs two hands and room; an automatic weapon can trade ammunition and its action for suppression. A dagger can work in a confined grapple where a long weapon cannot be aligned; a bow offers a quieter launch and recoverable arrows but needs two hands and drawing space. Apply the actual agreed range/handling, not invented post-roll bonuses. A free hand permits holding or an incidental interaction, never a second substantial action. Long-range disadvantage, cover and immediate melee pressure follow the profile; disadvantage does not stack. Insufficient reach or solid obstruction prevents the attack. Noise matters to actors who can actually hear and respond, not omniscient enemies.

If explicitly chosen at creation or advancement, a standard suppression profile uses one action and three rounds of ammunition, targeting one visible person or a narrow passage within normal effective range until the shooter's next turn. The targeted person's attacks through the swept area have disadvantage while exposed there; leaving it or complete cover avoids the effect. It deals no damage, grants no extra attack, does not freeze targets, bypass solid cover or affect distant unrelated enemies. Allies are not exempt if they enter the declared swept passage. Explain the area and effect before commitment. Other agreed weapon traits need equally clear resolution. Never grant suppression or a free burst to every existing automatic weapon retroactively.
