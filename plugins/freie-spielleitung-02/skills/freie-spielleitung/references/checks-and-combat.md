# Checks and combat

## Decide whether to roll

Roll uncertain actions with meaningful consequences. Clear actions succeed; impossible actions do not become possible through a lucky die. Use relevant expertise for basic knowledge. Chosen check frequency changes opportunities for risks, not DC or mandatory roll counts. Tone does not change numerical difficulty.

For persuasion, distinguish a person already willing to act from an uncertain attempt to overcome resistance. An eloquent speech supplies arguments and leverage; it does not alone establish that a listener abandons revenge, power, safety or loyalty. When the response remains uncertain and consequential, announce one fitting check before deciding it. On failure, enact the announced refusal, cost or escalation rather than praising the speech into success. Do not add a roll to a settled agreement, make a roll bypass a person's actual limits, or roll simply because no dice have appeared recently.

Before a check, state the intended objective, attribute, applicable talent/equipment, DC and foreseeable stakes in a meta note using the selected language. Do not reveal hidden facts just to announce risk. Preparation must have a stated effect: it permits the action, lowers DC, or gives advantage. Name that effect when announcing the check; if DC is lower, show the change when the baseline is known, otherwise explain that the chosen DC already includes the preparation. Praise alone is not a mechanical effect. Preparation that removes meaningful uncertainty can remove the need to roll. Genuine help can do the same; no invented support +2. Count a cause once.

Resolve the whole declared action. Use multiple stages only if they involve distinct decisions/stakes, not every grip and step. A successful preparation does not merely unlock another identical check; carry its effect into the actual objective. If the player declares several routine actions, compress them until the next meaningful uncertainty or decision.

## Formula and dice

D20 + attribute + one applicable talent (+2) + one equipment bonus (at most +2) ≥ DC: success.

| DC | Difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| 8 | easy |
| 11 | normal |
| 14 | demanding |
| 17 | hard |
| 20 | exceptional |

Use the table as the baseline. If actual preparation or circumstances change a task DC, show the baseline and reason (for example 14 → 11 after proper preparation). Do not silently replace it with arbitrary intermediate task DCs. Target defense is a separate derived value and can legitimately be 12, 13, 15 or another intermediate number; label it defense, not a new task difficulty.

Advantage: 2D20, take the higher. Disadvantage: 2D20, take the lower. Multiple sources do not stack; advantage and disadvantage cancel. No double reward/penalty for one cause. Natural 1/20 receive plausible effects, not automatic death, impossible feats or unannounced critical damage.

With player rolls, request raw dice for their character, wait, then calculate; do not request a pre-added total or invent the player’s raw roll. Keep this division after downtime and in long noncombat sequences too; clarify an ambiguous supplied total rather than adding its bonuses again. The GM handles NPCs/enemies using an actual random tool if available. Without one, label chat rolls as simulated once. Never pretend a tool was used, alter a result or reroll for a preferred outcome.

Open display includes enemies and raw arithmetic separately from fiction, e.g. “D20 12+Dexterity 3+Talent 2=17 ≥ DC14: success.” Show both advantage/disadvantage dice. With compact display, show outcome and changes; provide details on request. The previously announced stakes and DC remain binding. Equality is success: a total of 14 against defense 14 hits. A natural 1 does not authorize an unannounced damage rule or arbitrary catastrophe; apply the agreed check and its stated plausible consequences.

## Failure and retries

Failure changes the situation according to the announced stakes: harm, lost time, exposure, spent resources, changed position, relationship damage, a missed opportunity, or success at a price. Apply the consequence promptly and continue from the changed state. Failure can end an attempt or an adventure. Do not replace it with a new prerequisite and another near-identical roll until safe success is inevitable.

Same-method retries are allowed when fiction permits. A missed rope throw can be thrown again while the victim continues sinking; the throw need not be cosmetically redesigned. Each attempt costs the time/action/resources the situation requires, and consequences accumulate. If nothing can change and no consequence is possible, do not farm rolls: resolve the routine or state why no further attempt is available. A definitive failure may close an opportunity; a retry is not an entitlement.

When the announced consequence is eventual success at a time/resource cost, apply that cost and finish the declared action without another roll. Otherwise stop before committing the player to a new approach or further risk. If preparation, help or a resisting actor's actual behaviour changes the situation, update uncertainty and stakes visibly. Preserve the preparation already completed instead of resetting it. Distinct phases need distinct decisions or consequences; do not turn one repair into a sequence of hidden prerequisites. Do not invent and execute the character's improved solution after a failed check. NPCs may suggest or carry out their own plausible actions without choosing for the player.

Apply meaningful harm, loss and death when the fiction and resolution warrant them, in both Standard and Hardcore. State foreseeable stakes for the character before a roll; this does not require advance consent to every hostile event or disclosure of hidden threats. Resolve surprises through the actual situation and applicable checks, not automatic defeat. Preserve the result of a check and the actual benefit of precautions. New events or enemy actions may create new danger, but do not retroactively change announced stakes, an established anchor or a completed success. Correct an inconsistent ruling and restore unfair costs.

## Combat

NPCs can initiate combat, including an ambush or attack during conversation. Fighting, negotiation, flight, deception and surrender are attempts under actual circumstances, not guaranteed exits. Decide behaviour from motives and capabilities rather than protecting either side. Explain relevant positions, cover, reach and visible intentions before consequential choices.

### Establish the encounter

Fix each active creature's HP, attack bonus, defense, damage, movement and special effects before resolving its attacks. Retain remaining turns and temporary-effect expiry as well as HP; a quiet ally still guards, moves, acts or deliberately waits for a stated reason rather than disappearing from the encounter. In open display, show these values for visible combatants and remaining HP after damage, including companions. Hide genuinely unknown abilities until relevant; do not invent or adjust values to fit a roll. For newly designed ordinary humanoid opposition, 4 HP (fragile), 6 HP (trained) and 10 HP (formidable) are useful baselines, not universal species values. Justify stronger creatures and substantial exceptions. Assess numbers, actions, damage and the hero's available capabilities together; describe recognizable overwhelming danger without making every fight fair or automatically scaling foes to the hero.

Default defense = 10+Dexterity, plus 1 for appropriate armor. Each round grants one action and movement. Unless the creature or terrain establishes otherwise, ordinary movement is up to 10 m; using the action to dash adds up to 10 m. An ordinary melee attack needs plausible weapon reach (usually 1–2 m), line of effect and an available action. An established obstruction or limited movement can reduce this. Opening a question does not spend movement. A simple incidental item interaction can accompany the turn if it does not itself resolve a substantial task. Removing an embedded projectile is not a free extra heal and does not invent a new injury penalty.

Use the fiction for a clear order; otherwise each participant rolls D20+Dexterity without talent/equipment bonuses, highest first. Reroll only tied participants. Keep that order until circumstances actually change. Finish all earlier turns before requesting the player's action and all later turns before the next round. Do not restart each round at the player or let one actor act twice. On interruption, retain round, current actor and remaining turns. A compact end-of-round summary of HP, active effects, positions and next actor is enough; ordinary narration need not repeat every stat.

### Before each combat decision

Keep a small working combat state in accessible context: round/order/current actor, positions, current/max HP, defense, attack formula, damage band, active effects and relevant ammunition/resources. In open display, put visible targets' current/max HP and defense before asking for a tactical choice, not only after the player has selected a small weak point. A known ordinary hit that can incapacitate must be recognizable from those values; hidden anatomy can remain hidden. Refresh changed values, not the whole rules explanation.

Use this resolution gate for every actor: announce target + complete attribute/talent/equipment arithmetic + target defense + advantage/disadvantage and consequences; for the player obtain their raw attack roll and wait; for an NPC resolve its own attack without asking permission or requesting a player roll (label the first tool-free roll as simulated); determine hit; resolve selected damage dice before subtracting HP; update state; advance to the next actor. Do not abbreviate away the attribute or decide an NPC hit in prose. A failed attack deals no effective damage: a nearby shot may meet armor, cover or evasion rather than fly absurdly wide. Close range alone is not an automatic hit. Deliberately aiming at a small component needs a stated useful effect and difficulty before commitment; do not demand it for damage already possible with an ordinary attack.

### Resolve a turn

Advance opponents and allies through their own turns. They pursue their tasks rather than waiting for another player preparation roll. A declared approach plus action can resolve in the same turn within its allowances. Only roll separately for an obstacle with distinct stakes. Opponents may move or block on their turns, but cannot indefinitely deny an established reachable position by narration alone.

Attacks use the normal formula against target defense. Announce target, bonus, defense, preparation and foreseeable effects; obtain player raw rolls only for their character; resolve NPC rolls yourself. On a hit resolve damage through Optional Dice before showing remaining HP. No routine second defensive roll, free counterattack on a miss or extra attack from holding two weapons. A special protective action resolves its announced danger once rather than rolling again to undo that result. Assistance uses enabling, lower DC or advantage and costs a plausible action.

Cover that obscures a reachable target normally gives disadvantage to the attack; a solid obstruction blocking line of effect prevents it. Do not invent a flat defense bonus. A body does not automatically provide safe cover or transfer a missed shot into an automatic hit on someone else. State any actual collateral risk before the roll. Choosing to guard or evade as the action gives disadvantage to attacks the character can perceive until their next turn; ordinary narration of trying to dodge is already represented by defense and grants no free extra roll. A different agreed ability takes priority.

Retreat uses movement and, if chosen, the dash action. Do not add free opportunity attacks unless an agreed ability/rule provides them; enemies can pursue or attack on their normal turns. Resolve every attack still able to harm a target openly, including pursuit fire. Once range or cover makes harm impossible, describe ineffective fire without a roll. Establish mount/vehicle target values rather than using the rider's defense. Do not narrate another damaging attack after a check already resolved that danger.

Base damage is light 2, ordinary 3, heavy 4 for both sides; agreed exceptional powers or item effects can differ. No unannounced critical multipliers.

## Incapacitation, injuries and recovery

At 0 HP a creature is incapacitated. Determine and state whether the established attack and circumstances actually killed them or left them alive; 0 alone is not automatic death. A lethal injury, fire, predation or a deliberate finishing attack can kill. Explain foreseeable lethal danger before a risky check. Apply the selected death/rewind procedure only after actual death. Do not preserve every defeated enemy or guarantee capture, rescue or survival.

A living incapacitated creature needs stabilization and safe care before resting. Ordinary first aid with suitable means and no meaningful uncertainty succeeds; an uncertain urgent rescue uses a check with a specific stated consequence. Stabilization removes the immediate dying danger without restoring HP. Do not invent a separate sequence of rolls for every bandage or a hidden bleeding clock. Once stable, use the same recovery as for other creatures.

Default recovery is symmetric for player characters, companions and enemies: a safe uninterrupted short rest of at least one hour restores +2 HP once between full rests; a safe uninterrupted rest of at least eight hours restores full HP. A stabilized creature can recover this way from 0 HP. Ability resources recover according to their confirmed sheet; if an NPC resource is relevant, define its recovery too. A watch schedule can permit full rest when the creature actually gets the required sleep. First aid does not create extra HP unless an agreed healing effect says so.

Ordinary wounds and pain explain lost HP; they impose no separate penalty or slower recovery. After HP recovery, residual marks or soreness need not hinder play. Carry recovery through compressed days for NPCs as well as the hero; do not repeatedly describe a disabling wound without applying elapsed care and the agreed recovery. Lasting injuries are an optional agreed rule: define trigger, effect, treatment and recovery before using it, apply it consistently to relevant creatures, and distinguish it from HP. Do not suddenly keep NPCs crippled for days while the same wounds disappear overnight for the hero. Default play adds no infection, mandatory surgery or wound-treatment minigame.

## Powers, damage and the environment

Apply the confirmed ability's stated effect and cost. For a damaging spell or other power, use the normal D20 + relevant attribute + one talent + one equipment bonus against target defense unless the agreed ability specifies a different check. Use the same default damage bands as weapons: light 2, ordinary 3, heavy 4. These are defaults, not a universal limit on explicitly agreed exceptional powers. State and justify exceptional damage/scale before use. Define target count/area, range, cost and any persistence on the sheet before confirmation; do not improvise a free area attack or silently strip an agreed one down to harmless utility.

An area power costs its agreed action and resources once per use, not once per victim. For an ordinary instantaneous area attack, make one attack roll and compare its total with each exposed target's defense; apply the agreed damage to those hit, respecting any separately agreed resolution. Affected allies are not exempt unless the ability says so. State relevant cover, exposure, targets and foreseeable collateral danger before the player commits. A missed attack does not itself reverse a separately established environmental effect; nor does a damaging attack ignite every nearby object automatically. Fire spreads through suitable fuel and conditions over time; falling debris, smoke and other hazards follow coherent causes. Explain any additional hazard mechanic before enforcing it. Use one defined resolution per consequence rather than a second arbitrary roll to undo success. Fixed damage and any selected damage dice apply consistently to spells and weapons in the relevant damage band.

Keep narrative scope distinct from a numeric check bonus. A powerful ability can change what is possible, remove the need for a roll or reach more targets without granting an invented universal +2. Ordinary success remains possible without a new disadvantage invented to neutralize the power. Apply costs even on failure when the confirmed ability says so; clarify an unspecified material cost or resolution before commitment.

## Optional dice

At combat setup retain the selected dice by damage category. After each hit, if that category's die is selected, request the player's raw damage die and stop before damage, incapacitation, or the next actor's turn; obtain NPC damage through the same available-tool/simulation procedure as their attacks. One message may request attack and damage raw dice together only if it explicitly says the damage die is used on a hit; ignore it on a miss. Never apply base damage first and roll later. A selected D6 means an ordinary hit requires D6 resolution for every side, including spells. If only D6 is selected, heavy damage remains fixed 4; weapon profiles do not activate unselected dice. For an instantaneous area attack roll damage once per use and apply it to all targets hit unless the confirmed power states otherwise.

Use only selected extra dice; otherwise fixed base damage. Explain purpose/table before rolling. Follow the chosen roller/display rules, without attribute/talent modifiers for these extra rolls. Attribute checks remain D20.

- Selected D4 for light, D6 for ordinary, D8 for heavy damage: if roll ≤ floor(W/4), base−1; if roll > W−floor(W/4), base+1; otherwise base. Apply symmetrically to both sides. D4: 1→1 damage, 2–3→2, 4→3. D6: 1→2 damage, 2–5→3, 6→4. D8: 1–2→3 damage, 3–6→4, 7–8→5. These are variations around base damage, not full raw-die damage and not a new damage-0 system. Selecting one damage die does not activate the other categories.
- Selected D10: ten similarly valuable finds, with 0=10 when rolled alone.
- Selected D12: twelve world events, not mandatory detours to extend an adventure.
- Selected D% requires D10: tens 00–90 plus units 0–9; 00+0=100. Announce the chance first; result ≤ chance triggers the event.

Use selected find/event/chance dice when there is a genuine unresolved choice among suitable outcomes, not only in combat. Establish the table or chance before requesting the roll; do not use them to redraw a fixed investigation truth, contradict successful avoidance or create a mandatory extra adventure. Selection makes the option available, not a per-adventure quota.

## Slim weapon profiles

A newly created weapon can carry `weapon:{damage,range,handling,feature,limits}` in inventory. Damage is light/ordinary/heavy; the other fields describe effective reach, hands/space, one useful distinction and its cost or restriction. These fields are authoritative for that agreed item. General description, quantity, carrying slots and check bonus remain separate. Empty/0 weapon range is not unlimited reach. A profile never activates a die not selected by the player. A separately agreed item damage modifier is stated in feature, applied once in addition to the band result (after its selected die variation), and shown in arithmetic; it does not increase the check bonus or create another attack. Existing confirmed weapons without profiles keep their stated properties; do not infer a new template from the name. Agree missing consequential properties before their first relevant use.

As creation proposals, a pistol is compact and one-handed with moderate short-range damage; a shotgun is heavy close-range single-target damage with bulk and reload costs; a rifle can cover greater distance but needs two hands and room; an automatic weapon can trade ammunition and its action for suppression. A dagger can work in a confined grapple where a long weapon cannot be aligned; a bow offers a quieter launch and recoverable arrows but needs two hands and drawing space. Apply the actual agreed range/handling, not invented post-roll bonuses. A free hand permits holding or an incidental interaction, never a second substantial action. Long-range disadvantage, cover and immediate melee pressure follow the profile; disadvantage does not stack. Insufficient reach or solid obstruction prevents the attack. Noise matters to actors who can actually hear and respond, not omniscient enemies.

If explicitly chosen at creation or advancement, a standard suppression profile uses one action and three rounds of ammunition, targeting one visible person or a narrow passage within normal effective range until the shooter's next turn. The targeted person's attacks through the swept area have disadvantage while exposed there; leaving it or complete cover avoids the effect. It deals no damage, grants no extra attack, does not freeze targets, bypass solid cover or affect distant unrelated enemies. Allies are not exempt if they enter the declared swept passage. Explain the area and effect before commitment. Other agreed weapon traits need equally clear resolution. Never grant suppression or a free burst to every existing automatic weapon retroactively.
